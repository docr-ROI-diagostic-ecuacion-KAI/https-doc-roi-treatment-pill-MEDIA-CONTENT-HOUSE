THE LEARNING LOGIC

A Content House is not just a landing page. It is an instrumented learning environment.

The purpose of this pill is to teach students how content becomes observable marketing behaviour. We begin with a Buyer Persona and a product proposition, publish four Marketing Mix content frames, capture acquisition context through UTM parameters and observe consented interactions through first-party identifiers and strategic tags.

4 · UTMWhere did the visitor come from and at what acquisition cost?
5 · First-party IDWhich consented browser is returning?
6 · Tags & EventsWhich strategic proposition did the visitor engage with?
7 · CohortsHow large and costly is each behavioural audience?
01

Content becomes evidence

The four frames — Product, Price, Place and Promotion — are not decorative blocks. Each one represents a strategic hypothesis about what may matter to the visitor.

02

Tags make behaviour analysable

Every clickable proposition carries a marketing dimension and a strategic keyword. The click therefore becomes structured behavioural data instead of an anonymous page view.

03

Cohorts connect interest and cost

When tags are combined with UTM and acquisition cost, we can group visitors into behavioural cohorts and compare audience size, recency, frequency and acquisition economics.

Why build a Media Content House?

Traditional content publishing tells us what we sent to the market. An instrumented Content House adds a second layer: it tells us which strategic proposition each consented visitor actually consumed.

  • UTM preserves acquisition origin and campaign context.
  • First-party identity lets us recognise a consented returning browser without relying on third-party cookies.
  • Tags encode the meaning of a click: Product, Price, Place or Promotion plus a strategic keyword.
  • Events create a row-level behavioural history in the data layer.
  • Cohorts convert event history into audiences that can later support inbound and nurturing decisions.

Media Content House

A branded content environment designed not only to inform, but also to produce structured behavioural signals.

UTM

URL parameters that identify traffic source, medium, campaign, creative and keyword context.

First-party cookie

A cookie created by the domain the visitor is using. In this pill it stores a pseudonymous visitor identifier only after consent.

Visitor ID

A random pseudonymous identifier used to recognise the same consented browser across visits.

Session ID

A temporary identifier used to group actions that occur within the same browser session.

Strategic tag

A structured label attached to content so a click can be interpreted in marketing terms.

Marketing dimension

The high-level behavioural category used here: Home_Product, Home_Price, Home_Place or Home_Promotion.

Event

A timestamped interaction sent to the data collector, such as page_view or marketing_frame_click.

Cohort

A group of visitors who share a defined behavioural characteristic, such as repeated interest in price.

POES

Paid, Owned, Earned and Shared media — the channel logic used to classify acquisition activity.

Cost per visit

The attributable acquisition cost associated with bringing one visit into the Content House.

Collector endpoint

The secure web address that receives browser events and writes them to Google Sheets, Excel or another approved data store.

GDPR-oriented Consent & First-party Identity

The generated landing page must not create the analytics/personalisation identifier or transmit behavioural events until the visitor actively accepts.

Educational and technical guidance, not legal certification. A first-party cookie used for analytics or commercial personalisation may require consent and appropriate transparency. Production deployment should be reviewed against the organisation's legal basis, privacy notice, retention policy and processor arrangements.
The prompt will require Accept, Reject and Manage / Privacy controls.

Data Destination & Event Collection

This step answers the question that usually causes confusion: where will the event go, and how will it reach the spreadsheet?

Simple rule. The landing page should not log into a private spreadsheet directly. It sends an event to a controlled collector. The collector then writes the row into Google Sheets or Excel.
If you do not have it yet, the generated prompt will leave a clearly marked COLLECTOR_ENDPOINT placeholder.
Recommended for this pill: Google Sheets + Google Apps Script Web App.

The landing page sends a POST request to the deployed Apps Script URL. The script appends the event to Event_Log. No Google password or API secret is placed in the HTML.

METHODOLOGY AT THE END OF THE JOURNEY

DIIIP explains why this connection creates value

The Content House is not valuable because a page has been published. Value appears when raw acquisition and interaction data are transformed into visible decisions about audiences, content and personalisation.

D
Data

UTM parameters, consented visitor ID, session ID, page views and clicks on Product, Price, Place and Promotion content.

I
Information

Structured event rows containing acquisition source, campaign, timestamp, strategic keyword, marketing dimension and cost per visit.

I
Intelligence

The event history is organised into behavioural patterns: which propositions attract attention, which visitors return and which channels produce those behaviours.

I
Insights

Audience size by cohort, recency, frequency, acquisition cost and the relative strength of Product, Price, Place and Promotion signals.

P
Personalization Actions

Use those insights to decide what content, landing experience, inbound sequence or nurturing proposition should come next.
